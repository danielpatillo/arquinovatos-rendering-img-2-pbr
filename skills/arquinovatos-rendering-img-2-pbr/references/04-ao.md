# Generate a Seamless PBR Ambient Occlusion Map

## 1. Objective

Generate an Ambient Occlusion Map that estimates local geometric obstruction to ambient illumination.

The map must describe the material's cavities, confined regions, and nearby surface interactions—not the lighting arrangement in the photograph.

Reference-guided geometric estimation is authorized. Proceed without requesting additional approval because an exact source mesh is unavailable.

## 2. Reference Hierarchy

### Image 1 — Master Albedo and UV Layout

Use Image 1 as the authoritative reference for the position, shape, proportions, orientation, and distribution of features.

Preserve its dimensions, framing, and nominal coverage of 2 × 2 meters.

### Image 2 — Surface Geometry Reference

Use Image 2 to interpret which features are concave, confined, overlapping, open, shallow, or deeply recessed.

Do not transfer its perspective, cast shadows, directional lighting, or feature positions.

Image 1 determines WHERE features belong. Image 2 guides their inferred OCCLUSION GEOMETRY.

## 3. AO Meaning and Encoding

Use the standard convention:
- White, 1: no occlusion within the evaluated neighborhood.
- Black, 0: maximum occlusion.
- Intermediate gray: partial occlusion.

White does not mean that the surface always receives direct light.

Black does not mean that the surface can never receive direct light.

Represent environmental accessibility caused by geometry, independently of any particular light direction.

## 4. Required Construction Method

First establish a coherent interpretation of the material's local relief.

Estimate occlusion from the relationship between feature depth, width, enclosure, proximity, and surrounding geometry.

Narrow, deep cavities may require more occlusion than broad, shallow depressions.

Open surfaces should remain close to white when nearby geometry does not meaningfully obstruct them.

Do not turn every joint into a black line or outline every surface feature.

Do not generate AO by simply inverting the albedo or height map. Low elevation alone does not determine occlusion.

If an approved height map or geometric representation is actually available, use it to improve consistency. Otherwise, produce a conservative estimate from the two references without claiming a true mesh bake.

## 5. Separate Occlusion From Color and Lighting

Do not interpret dark pigmentation as occlusion.

Do not copy cast shadows, specular highlights, exposure gradients, or photographic vignetting.

Use Image 2's shading only as contextual evidence about shape—not as a shadow pattern to reproduce.

Do not restore dirt, stains, or damage removed from Image 1.

Preserve genuine cavities and surface confinement without inventing new geometry.

## 6. Occlusion Radius, Contrast, and Strength

Use a local occlusion scale appropriate to the material's pores, joints, and relief.

Avoid an excessively broad radius that produces large, muddy patches unrelated to local surface confinement.

Occlusion radius and visual intensity are different properties. A later strength adjustment cannot correct an inappropriate spatial radius.

Preserve intermediate values and gradual transitions.

Reserve very dark values for strongly confined regions. Do not force pure black into partially open cavities.

A mostly exposed material may legitimately produce a predominantly white map. Do not add artificial darkness merely to increase contrast.

The user will adjust the final AO influence in the renderer.

## 7. Seamless Continuity and Independence

Treat the material as periodically repeated beyond the image boundaries.

Include neighboring repetitions when evaluating edge features so occlusion does not change artificially at the tile borders.

Maintain exact registration with Image 1.

Do not introduce dark frames, bright border strips, seam-specific halos, or geometric distortions.

Keep AO separate from the albedo, roughness, and specular maps. Do not permanently multiply it into those files.

## 8. Output, Validation, and Delivery

- Map: Ambient Occlusion only.
- Format: lossless single-channel grayscale PNG.
- Precision: 8 bits per channel.
- Dimensions and orientation: identical to Image 1.
- Interpretation: non-color scalar data; no sRGB decoding.
- Filename: Material_AO.png.

Check that dark regions correspond to geometric confinement rather than pigment or photographic shadows.

Verify alignment and periodic continuity where tools permit. Do not claim checks or a geometric bake that were not performed.

Return only the completed AO map, without labels, a render, or other maps.

Briefly disclose any technical limitation outside the image, without requesting approval for estimation already authorized.
